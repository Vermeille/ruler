# Employment and occupations

[I] EMPLOYMENT-WAGE1: a minimum wage above local payroll capacity reduces the share of viable jobs; stronger sector output and healthier workers support higher wages while business taxes reduce payroll capacity.

[I] EMPLOYMENT-DEMAND1: the number of employed adults local firms want is limited by occupational job viability and falls when businesses are weak, business taxes are high, or food security is poor.

[I] EMPLOYMENT-LAYOFF1: when jobs disappear, workers in less viable sectors with less education and less adaptable archetypes lose employment first.

[I] EMPLOYMENT-HIRING1: when jobs become available, residents in viable sectors with more education and more adaptable archetypes find employment first.

[I] OCCUPATION-ENTRY1: working-age adults without an occupation choose the locally strongest sector after weighting opportunities by their archetype's sector affinities.

[I] OCCUPATION-OPPORTUNITY1: local occupational opportunity reflects the existing workforce, sector-specific local conditions and market signals, subsidies, job viability, and expected sector output.

[I] RETRAINING-CADENCE1: adults reconsider their occupation every six months when another sector offers a materially better local opportunity.

[I] RETRAINING-EMPLOYED1: employed adults switch sectors gradually only when the alternative is at least fifteen percent better; adaptability and education access make switching more likely.

[I] RETRAINING-UNEMPLOYED1: unemployed adults retrain more aggressively toward better opportunities, with adaptability and education access increasing the retraining flow.
